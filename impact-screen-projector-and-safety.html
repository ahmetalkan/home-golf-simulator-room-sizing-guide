Impact Screen Buffer, Ricochet Prevention and Projector Placement

A solid golf ball coming off a driver or low wedge carries tremendous kinetic energy. Proper enclosure design absorbs that impact quietly instead of firing the ball back toward the tee.

Five bay-safety essentials

  • 12-to-18-inch air gap behind the impact screen: Keeps deflected screen fabric from slamming into drywall or concrete blocks, which both damages walls and rebounds balls forcefully.
  • Do not drum-tighten the screen: Slight give lets multi-layer impact fabric deaden ball speed so shots drop gently onto the turf instead of bouncing back at face height.
  • Pad every exposed pipe and ceiling corner: Use high-density foam inserts and angled side-baffle curtains to catch hosel shanks and high-lofted lob wedges.
  • Protect the projector with short-throw mounting: Position a short-throw projector ahead of the swing plane—or slightly behind and protected by a floor enclosure or ceiling cage—and route power cords cleanly per OSHA electrical safety basics.
  • Clear spectator zone: Keep guests seated well behind the hitting mat outside the club-arc radius.
